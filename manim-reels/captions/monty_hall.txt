In 1990, Marilyn vos Savant answered a reader's puzzle in Parade magazine: you pick one of three doors, the host (who knows where the car is) opens a goat door, and offers you a switch. Her answer: always switch. You win 2/3 of the time.

She received around 10,000 letters, many from people with PhDs, telling her she was wrong. Even Paul Erdős reportedly wasn't convinced until he saw a computer simulation.

The key: the host's choice is not random. He can never reveal the car, so his action carries information, and it concentrates the original 2/3 onto the one door he leaves closed.

Source: Jason Rosenhouse, The Monty Hall Problem (Oxford University Press, 2009).
